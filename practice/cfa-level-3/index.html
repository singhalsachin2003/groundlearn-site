Asset Allocation

15–20% of the exam

Capital market expectations, allocation principles, real-world constraints.

Question 1

An analyst uses the Grinold–Kroner model with an expected dividend yield of 2.2%, expected share buyback of 0.8%, inflation of 2.0%, real earnings growth of 1.8% and no repricing. The expected equity return is closest to:

D/P 2.2% · −ΔS 0.8% · i 2.0% · g 1.8% · ΔP/E 0%
  1. A4.8%
  2. B6.0%
  3. C6.8%
  4. D8.6%
Show the answer

C. 6.8%

2.2 + 0.8 + 2.0 + 1.8 + 0 = 6.8%. Net share buyback is added because it raises per-share claims on earnings.

Curriculum area: Asset Allocation — Capital Market Expectations, Part 2

Question 2

An investor with several distinct, prioritised objectives and different time horizons is best served by:

  1. AAsset-only mean–variance optimisation
  2. BLiability-relative allocation
  3. CGoals-based allocation
  4. DA single risk-parity portfolio
Show the answer

C. Goals-based allocation

Goals-based allocation builds a sub-portfolio for each goal with its own horizon and required probability of success.

Curriculum area: Asset Allocation — Overview of Asset Allocation

Question 3

The principal criticism of unconstrained mean–variance optimisation is that it:

  1. ACannot handle more than two assets
  2. BProduces highly concentrated portfolios sensitive to input estimation error
  3. CIgnores correlation between assets
  4. DRequires normally distributed returns to be computed at all
Show the answer

B. Produces highly concentrated portfolios sensitive to input estimation error

MVO is an error-maximiser: small changes in expected return inputs produce large swings in optimal weights, typically toward concentration.

Curriculum area: Asset Allocation — Principles of Asset Allocation

Question 4

Which factor argues most strongly for a wider rebalancing corridor around a target allocation?

  1. AHigher asset class volatility
  2. BHigher transaction costs
  3. CHigher correlation with the rest of the portfolio
  4. DA shorter investment horizon
Show the answer

B. Higher transaction costs

Higher transaction costs make frequent rebalancing expensive, justifying a wider corridor. Higher volatility argues for a narrower one.

Curriculum area: Asset Allocation — Asset Allocation with Real-World Constraints

Question 5

An analyst forecasts long-run returns using only the most recent five years of data during a strong bull market. This most likely reflects:

  1. AThe ex post risk trap
  2. BStatus quo bias
  3. CData measurement error
  4. DModel uncertainty
Show the answer

A. The ex post risk trap

The ex post risk trap treats a period of realised high returns and low volatility as representative, understating forward-looking risk.

Curriculum area: Asset Allocation — Capital Market Expectations, Part 1

Portfolio Construction

15–20% of the exam

Equity and fixed-income portfolios, alternatives, institutions, trading costs.

Question 6

A portfolio has an active share of 0.75 but active risk of only 2%. This combination most likely describes:

Active share 0.75 · Active risk 2%
  1. AA concentrated sector-rotation strategy
  2. BA diversified stock picker with limited factor tilts
  3. CA closet indexer
  4. DA leveraged long-short fund
Show the answer

B. A diversified stock picker with limited factor tilts

High active share means very different holdings; low active risk means those differences largely offset, which is diversified stock selection.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Construction — Overview of Equity Portfolio Management

Question 7

In immunising a single liability, the manager should match the present value and duration of the liability while:

  1. AMaximising portfolio convexity
  2. BMinimising portfolio convexity
  3. CMaximising portfolio yield
  4. DMatching the liability's cash flows exactly
Show the answer

B. Minimising portfolio convexity

Minimising convexity subject to matching duration minimises structural risk from non-parallel shifts in the yield curve.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Construction — Overview of Fixed-Income Portfolio Management

Question 8

An endowment with a perpetual horizon and a 5% spending rule is best positioned to bear:

  1. ALow liquidity risk and high liability risk
  2. BHigh illiquidity and equity risk relative to most institutions
  3. CRegulatory capital constraints similar to an insurer
  4. DA duration-matched fixed income mandate
Show the answer

B. High illiquidity and equity risk relative to most institutions

A perpetual horizon and predictable spending permit substantial allocations to illiquid and equity-like assets in pursuit of the return required.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Construction — Portfolio Management for Institutional Investors

Question 9

An order for 10,000 shares is placed at a decision price of $50. 8,000 shares fill at an average of $50.40 and the order is cancelled with the price at $51. Implementation shortfall includes:

Decision $50 · 8,000 @ $50.40 · 2,000 unfilled, price $51
  1. AOnly the realised price impact on the 8,000 shares
  2. BRealised impact plus commissions only
  3. CRealised impact, commissions and the opportunity cost on the 2,000 unfilled shares
  4. DOnly the commission paid
Show the answer

C. Realised impact, commissions and the opportunity cost on the 2,000 unfilled shares

Implementation shortfall captures the full gap between the paper portfolio and the actual one, including the cost of the portion never executed.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Construction — Trading Costs and Electronic Markets

Question 10

Compared with a defined benefit pension plan, a sovereign wealth fund most distinctively faces:

  1. AA contractual liability stream
  2. BA public policy mandate and political scrutiny
  3. CA regulatory solvency ratio
  4. DMandatory annual distributions
Show the answer

B. A public policy mandate and political scrutiny

Sovereign wealth funds typically have no contractual liability but must operate under a public mandate with political and transparency constraints.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Construction — Portfolio Management for Institutional Investors

Performance Measurement

5–10% of the exam

Attribution, appraisal, manager selection and the GIPS standards.

Question 11

A manager overweighted a sector that outperformed the benchmark but selected stocks within it that underperformed. Brinson attribution will show:

  1. APositive allocation and positive selection
  2. BPositive allocation and negative selection
  3. CNegative allocation and positive selection
  4. DNegative allocation and negative selection
Show the answer

B. Positive allocation and negative selection

Sector weighting decisions land in allocation; within-sector security decisions land in selection. The two can and often do have opposite signs.

Curriculum area: Performance Measurement — Portfolio Performance Evaluation

Question 12

An investor allocates to one of many specialist managers within a diversified programme. The most appropriate appraisal measure is:

  1. AThe Sharpe ratio
  2. BThe information ratio
  3. CTotal return
  4. DThe Sortino ratio using total risk
Show the answer

B. The information ratio

For a benchmarked sleeve within a larger programme, active return per unit of active risk — the information ratio — is the relevant measure.

Curriculum area: Performance Measurement — Investment Manager Selection

Question 13

Hiring a manager who subsequently proves to have no skill is best described as:

  1. AA Type I error
  2. BA Type II error
  3. CA style drift
  4. DA benchmark misfit
Show the answer

A. A Type I error

The null hypothesis is that the manager has no skill. Hiring anyway is rejecting a true null — a Type I error.

Curriculum area: Performance Measurement — Investment Manager Selection

Question 14

Under the GIPS standards, a firm claiming compliance must:

  1. AInclude all fee-paying discretionary portfolios in at least one composite
  2. BInclude only its flagship strategies
  3. CPresent a minimum of three years of history
  4. DExclude terminated portfolios from historical composites
Show the answer

A. Include all fee-paying discretionary portfolios in at least one composite

Firm-wide compliance requires every fee-paying discretionary portfolio to sit in a composite. Terminated portfolios must remain in the historical record.

Curriculum area: Performance Measurement — Overview of the GIPS Standards

Question 15

Which type of due diligence failure most commonly causes catastrophic investor loss?

  1. AInvestment due diligence
  2. BOperational due diligence
  3. CBenchmark selection
  4. DFee negotiation
Show the answer

B. Operational due diligence

Fraud, valuation failure and inadequate controls — operational issues — account for most total losses, despite investment analysis receiving more attention.

Curriculum area: Performance Measurement — Investment Manager Selection

Derivatives & Risk Management

10–15% of the exam

Options strategies, swaps/forwards/futures strategies, currency management.

Question 16

An investor buys stock at $48 and writes a call with a $52 strike for a $2 premium. The maximum gain on the covered call is:

S₀ = $48 · X = $52 · premium $2
  1. A$2
  2. B$4
  3. C$6
  4. DUnlimited
Show the answer

C. $6

Maximum gain = (X − S₀) + premium = (52 − 48) + 2 = $6, achieved at or above the strike. Upside beyond $52 is surrendered.

Curriculum area: Derivatives and Risk Management — Options Strategies

Question 17

A zero-cost collar on a long equity position produces:

  1. AUnlimited upside with limited downside
  2. BBounded upside and bounded downside
  3. CUnlimited downside with limited upside
  4. DThe same payoff as the underlying
Show the answer

B. Bounded upside and bounded downside

The long put floors the loss and the short call caps the gain. The premium received funds the premium paid, hence zero cost.

Curriculum area: Derivatives and Risk Management — Options Strategies

Question 18

A manager holds a $50m portfolio with a beta of 1.1 and wants a beta of 0.7. Futures with a beta of 0.95 have a contract value of $250,000. The required position is closest to:

V_P $50m · β_P 1.1 · β_T 0.7 · β_f 0.95 · P_f $250,000
  1. ABuy 84 contracts
  2. BSell 84 contracts
  3. CSell 21 contracts
  4. DBuy 21 contracts
Show the answer

B. Sell 84 contracts

N = [(0.7 − 1.1)/0.95] × (50,000,000/250,000) = −0.4211 × 200 = −84.2. The negative sign means selling roughly 84 contracts.

Curriculum area: Derivatives and Risk Management — Swaps, Forwards and Futures Strategies

Question 19

Which condition most strongly argues for leaving foreign currency exposure unhedged?

  1. AA short investment horizon
  2. BHigh correlation between the currency and the asset return
  3. CA long horizon and low correlation between currency and asset returns
  4. DHigh hedging cost combined with a short horizon
Show the answer

C. A long horizon and low correlation between currency and asset returns

Over long horizons currency effects tend to mean-revert, and low correlation means the currency contributes diversification rather than risk.

Curriculum area: Derivatives and Risk Management — Currency Management

Question 20

A domestic investor earns 8% on a foreign asset while the foreign currency appreciates 3%. The domestic-currency return is closest to:

R_FC = 8% · R_FX = 3%
  1. A5.00%
  2. B11.00%
  3. C11.24%
  4. D24.00%
Show the answer

C. 11.24%

(1.08)(1.03) − 1 = 1.1124 − 1 = 11.24%. Adding the two returns gives 11.00% and omits the cross-product term.

Curriculum area: Derivatives and Risk Management — Currency Management

Ethical & Professional Standards

10–15% of the exam

The Standards plus the Asset Manager Code — which appears only at Level III.

Question 21

A firm wishes to state that it complies with the Asset Manager Code. It may do so only if it:

  1. AAdopts the provisions it considers relevant to its business
  2. BAdopts the Code in its entirety
  3. CObtains CFA Institute pre-approval of its policies
  4. DEmploys at least one CFA charterholder
Show the answer

B. Adopts the Code in its entirety

The Asset Manager Code must be adopted in full. Partial adoption cannot be claimed, and this is the single most examinable fact in the reading.

Curriculum area: Ethics — Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct

Question 22

A manager directs client brokerage to a broker that provides research used across all client portfolios. Under Standard III(A) this is:

  1. AProhibited in all circumstances
  2. BPermitted, provided best execution is sought and the benefit accrues to clients
  3. CPermitted only if the client pre-approves each trade
  4. DPermitted only for institutional clients
Show the answer

B. Permitted, provided best execution is sought and the benefit accrues to clients

Client brokerage is a client asset. It may purchase research that benefits clients, subject to the duty to seek best execution.

Curriculum area: Standard III(A) — Loyalty, Prudence and Care

Question 23

A firm materially changes its investment process without informing existing clients. This most likely violates:

  1. AStandard V(B) Communication with Clients
  2. BStandard III(C) Suitability
  3. CStandard VI(C) Referral Fees
  4. DStandard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors
Show the answer

A. Standard V(B) Communication with Clients

Standard V(B) requires prompt disclosure of material changes to the investment process to all clients, not only prospective ones.

Curriculum area: Standard V(B) — Communication with Clients

Question 24

At Level III, suitability under Standard III(C) is assessed:

  1. ASecurity by security in isolation
  2. BIn the context of the client's total portfolio
  3. COnly at the time the IPS is written
  4. DBy reference to the benchmark alone
Show the answer

B. In the context of the client's total portfolio

A security that is risky in isolation may be entirely suitable within a diversified total portfolio. Suitability is a portfolio-level judgement.

Curriculum area: Standard III(C) — Suitability

Question 25

A manager uses a proprietary risk model with known limitations in stressed markets and does not mention them to clients. This is best described as:

  1. AAcceptable, since the model is proprietary
  2. BA violation of Standard V(B), which requires disclosure of significant limitations
  3. CA violation of Standard I(B) Independence
  4. DAcceptable if the model is independently validated
Show the answer

B. A violation of Standard V(B), which requires disclosure of significant limitations

Standard V(B) requires disclosure of the basic process and its significant limitations and inherent risks. Proprietary status does not exempt that duty.

Curriculum area: Standard V(B) — Communication with Clients

Portfolio Management pathway

30–35% of the exam

Active equity, yield-curve and credit strategy, LDI, trade execution.

Question 26

A manager expects the yield curve to steepen with short rates unchanged. The most appropriate position is:

  1. AExtend duration across the curve
  2. BBuy the short end and sell the long end
  3. CSell the short end and buy the long end
  4. DA duration-neutral butterfly
Show the answer

B. Buy the short end and sell the long end

Steepening means long yields rise relative to short yields, so long bonds lose. Overweight the short end and underweight the long end.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management Pathway — Yield Curve Strategies

Question 27

Relative to a duration-matched bullet, a barbell portfolio will outperform when the yield curve:

  1. AShifts in a parallel fashion
  2. BFlattens
  3. CSteepens
  4. DRemains unchanged
Show the answer

B. Flattens

A barbell has greater convexity and benefits from a flattening, since the long-end holding gains more than the short-end holding loses.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management Pathway — Yield Curve Strategies

Question 28

A credit portfolio has spread duration of 4.5 and spreads widen by 30 basis points over a year in which spread carry is 2.0%. Ignoring defaults, excess return is closest to:

SD 4.5 · Δs +30bp · carry 2.0%
  1. A−1.35%
  2. B+0.65%
  3. C+2.00%
  4. D+3.35%
Show the answer

B. +0.65%

EXR ≈ carry − (SD × Δs) = 2.00% − (4.5 × 0.30%) = 2.00% − 1.35% = +0.65%.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management Pathway — Fixed-Income Active Management: Credit Strategies

Question 29

Which execution benchmark is most vulnerable to manipulation by the trader being measured?

  1. AArrival price
  2. BVWAP computed over the trader's own participation window
  3. CPrevious close
  4. DImplementation shortfall
Show the answer

B. VWAP computed over the trader's own participation window

A trader who supplies a large share of volume in the measurement window influences the VWAP itself, weakening the benchmark.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management Pathway — Trade Strategy and Execution

Question 30

Compared with a fundamental concentrated strategy, a quantitative multifactor equity strategy typically exhibits:

  1. AHigher active share and higher idiosyncratic risk
  2. BLower active share and risk sourced primarily from factor tilts
  3. CIdentical active share and active risk
  4. DLower active risk and higher stock-specific risk
Show the answer

B. Lower active share and risk sourced primarily from factor tilts

Quantitative strategies hold many names with small deviations, giving lower active share, with risk dominated by systematic factor exposures.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management Pathway — Active Equity Investing: Portfolio Construction

Private Markets pathway

30–35% of the exam

GP/LP economics, private equity and debt, special situations, real assets.

Question 31

A fund has paid-in capital of $100m, distributions of $40m and a remaining net asset value of $90m. TVPI is:

PIC $100m · Distributions $40m · NAV $90m
  1. A0.40×
  2. B0.90×
  3. C1.30×
  4. D2.25×
Show the answer

C. 1.30×

DPI = 40/100 = 0.40×, RVPI = 90/100 = 0.90×, so TVPI = 0.40 + 0.90 = 1.30×.

Curriculum area: Private Markets Pathway — General Partner and Investor Perspectives

Question 32

Under a European (whole-fund) waterfall compared with an American (deal-by-deal) waterfall, the general partner receives carried interest:

  1. AEarlier, with a higher clawback risk
  2. BLater, with a lower clawback risk
  3. CEarlier, with a lower clawback risk
  4. DAt the same time under both structures
Show the answer

B. Later, with a lower clawback risk

A whole-fund waterfall returns all contributed capital and the preferred return before any carry is paid, which delays carry and reduces clawback risk.

Curriculum area: Private Markets Pathway — General Partner and Investor Perspectives

Question 33

A general partner uses a subscription-line credit facility to delay capital calls. The most direct effect on reported performance is:

  1. ATVPI rises and IRR is unchanged
  2. BIRR rises and TVPI is broadly unchanged or slightly lower
  3. CBoth IRR and TVPI rise
  4. DBoth measures fall
Show the answer

B. IRR rises and TVPI is broadly unchanged or slightly lower

Delaying the capital call shortens the period capital is outstanding, flattering the money-weighted IRR. Interest cost slightly reduces the multiple.

Curriculum area: Private Markets Pathway — General Partner and Investor Perspectives

Question 34

A covenant-lite loan compared with a traditional maintenance-covenant loan most likely implies:

  1. AHigher expected recovery for the lender
  2. BLower expected recovery, because intervention is delayed
  3. CNo difference in expected recovery
  4. DHigher recovery but greater default probability
Show the answer

B. Lower expected recovery, because intervention is delayed

Without maintenance covenants the lender cannot force early remediation, so deterioration runs further before workout, reducing recovery.

Curriculum area: Private Markets Pathway — Private Debt

Question 35

A brownfield infrastructure asset with a regulated revenue framework will most likely display:

  1. AEquity-like returns and high cyclical sensitivity
  2. BBond-like returns and low cyclical sensitivity
  3. CEquity-like returns and low cyclical sensitivity
  4. DHigh construction risk
Show the answer

B. Bond-like returns and low cyclical sensitivity

Operational assets with regulated or contracted revenue produce stable, long-duration cash flows that behave more like bonds than equities.

Curriculum area: Private Markets Pathway — Infrastructure

Private Wealth pathway

30–35% of the exam

Goals-based planning, tax efficiency, human capital, wealth transfer.

Question 36

A client has a high-priority goal three years away and a lower-priority aspirational goal thirty years away. Goals-based allocation would fund the near-term goal with:

  1. AA high-equity sub-portfolio to maximise expected value
  2. BA low-risk sub-portfolio regardless of overall risk tolerance
  3. CThe same allocation as the aspirational goal
  4. DIlliquid private assets for the return premium
Show the answer

B. A low-risk sub-portfolio regardless of overall risk tolerance

Each goal is funded according to its own horizon and required probability of success. A high-priority near-term goal requires low-risk assets.

Curriculum area: Private Wealth Pathway — Investment Planning

Question 37

A senior executive holds substantial employer stock and derives employment income from the same firm. The most appropriate recommendation is:

  1. AIncrease the employer stock position to align interests
  2. BReduce the concentration because human and financial capital are correlated
  3. CHold the position because it is tax-efficient
  4. DHedge only the employment income
Show the answer

B. Reduce the concentration because human and financial capital are correlated

Human capital and financial capital are highly correlated here, so a shock to the employer hits both simultaneously. Diversification is the remedy.

Curriculum area: Private Wealth Pathway — Wealth Planning

Question 38

An asset returns 6% annually and is taxed at 30% on an accrual basis. Over ten years the after-tax accumulation factor is closest to:

r = 6% · t = 30% · n = 10
  1. A1.34×
  2. B1.51×
  3. C1.79×
  4. D1.26×
Show the answer

B. 1.51×

After-tax return = 6% × (1 − 0.30) = 4.2%. (1.042)¹⁰ = 1.508×. Taxing only at the end would give a higher figure.

Curriculum area: Private Wealth Pathway — Preserving the Wealth

Question 39

Asset location refers to the decision about:

  1. AWhich countries to invest in
  2. BWhich account type holds each asset class
  3. CThe proportion held in each asset class
  4. DThe custodian used for each holding
Show the answer

B. Which account type holds each asset class

Asset location places heavily taxed assets in tax-advantaged accounts and tax-efficient assets in taxable accounts, without changing the allocation.

Curriculum area: Private Wealth Pathway — Preserving the Wealth

Question 40

A lifetime gift is most likely to dominate a bequest when the recipient has:

  1. AA higher tax rate and a short horizon
  2. BA lower tax rate and a long horizon
  3. CThe same tax rate and a short horizon
  4. DA higher tax rate and a long horizon
Show the answer

B. A lower tax rate and a long horizon

A lower recipient tax rate and a longer compounding period both raise the relative value of gifting today rather than transferring at death.

Curriculum area: Private Wealth Pathway — Transferring the Wealth
