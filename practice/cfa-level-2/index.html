Ethical & Professional Standards

10–15% of the exam

The same Standards, applied to dense vignettes with competing duties.

Question 1

An analyst assembles several individually immaterial non-public details from suppliers and customers and, combined with public filings, concludes the issuer will miss guidance. Acting on that conclusion is:

  1. AA violation of Standard II(A)
  2. BPermitted under the mosaic theory
  3. CPermitted only after disclosure to the issuer
  4. DA violation of Standard V(A)
Show the answer

B. Permitted under the mosaic theory

The mosaic theory expressly permits combining non-material non-public information with public information. No single piece was material on its own.

Curriculum area: Standard II(A) — Material Nonpublic Information

Question 2

A firm offers premium clients earlier access to research than standard clients, and discloses the tiering to all clients. Under Standard III(B) this is:

  1. AA violation, because all clients must receive research simultaneously
  2. BPermitted, because the differential service is disclosed
  3. CA violation, unless the premium fee is waived
  4. DPermitted only for institutional clients
Show the answer

B. Permitted, because the differential service is disclosed

Fair dealing does not require identical treatment. Different service levels are permissible provided they are disclosed and do not disadvantage other clients.

Curriculum area: Standard III(B) — Fair Dealing

Question 3

A manager presents a composite that excludes two terminated accounts that underperformed. This most likely violates:

  1. AStandard III(D) Performance Presentation
  2. BStandard VI(B) Priority of Transactions
  3. CStandard IV(A) Loyalty to Employer
  4. DStandard I(B) Independence and Objectivity
Show the answer

A. Standard III(D) Performance Presentation

Excluding terminated accounts creates survivorship bias and makes the presentation neither fair nor complete, breaching Standard III(D).

Curriculum area: Standard III(D) — Performance Presentation

Question 4

A member takes a research report prepared at her previous employer and reuses its analysis, with attribution removed, at her new firm. She has violated:

  1. AStandard IV(A) only
  2. BStandard I(C) Misrepresentation and Standard IV(A) Loyalty
  3. CStandard V(B) only
  4. DNo standard, because she wrote the original analysis
Show the answer

B. Standard I(C) Misrepresentation and Standard IV(A) Loyalty

The work product belongs to the former employer, and presenting it as new work without attribution is plagiarism under Standard I(C).

Curriculum area: Standard I(C) — Misrepresentation; Standard IV(A) — Loyalty

Question 5

An investment firm allocates a hot IPO pro rata across all suitable client accounts, including accounts in which employees are beneficial owners. Under Standard VI(B), this is:

  1. AAcceptable if client accounts are filled before employee-owned accounts
  2. BAcceptable in all circumstances
  3. CA violation regardless of the allocation order
  4. DAcceptable only if the employees are not portfolio managers
Show the answer

A. Acceptable if client accounts are filled before employee-owned accounts

Priority of transactions requires client interests to come before those of employees. Employee-beneficial accounts may participate only after clients are satisfied.

Curriculum area: Standard VI(B) — Priority of Transactions

Quantitative Methods

5–10% of the exam

Multiple regression, misspecification, time series, machine learning.

Question 6

A multiple regression reports a significant F-statistic but no individually significant t-statistics. This most likely indicates:

  1. AHeteroskedasticity
  2. BSerial correlation
  3. CMulticollinearity
  4. DA unit root
Show the answer

C. Multicollinearity

Multicollinearity inflates the standard errors of individual coefficients while leaving the joint explanatory power intact — exactly this signature.

Curriculum area: Quantitative Methods — Model Misspecification, LOS 3.b

Question 7

Adding a variable to a regression raises R² from 0.42 to 0.44 but lowers adjusted R² from 0.39 to 0.38. The analyst should conclude the variable:

R²: 0.42 → 0.44 · R̄²: 0.39 → 0.38
  1. AImproves the model and should be retained
  2. BDoes not justify its loss of degrees of freedom
  3. CIntroduces heteroskedasticity
  4. DMakes the model non-stationary
Show the answer

B. Does not justify its loss of degrees of freedom

R² always rises with an added regressor. A falling adjusted R² means the explanatory gain does not compensate for the lost degree of freedom.

Curriculum area: Quantitative Methods — Evaluating Regression Model Fit, LOS 2.c

Question 8

An AR(1) model is estimated on a series with a unit root. The most appropriate remedy is to:

  1. AAdd a lag to the model
  2. BFirst-difference the series
  3. CUse White-corrected standard errors
  4. DApply a log transformation to the dependent variable
Show the answer

B. First-difference the series

A unit root violates covariance stationarity and produces spurious results. First differencing typically restores stationarity.

Curriculum area: Quantitative Methods — Time-Series Analysis, LOS 5.f

Question 9

Conditional heteroskedasticity in a regression most directly causes:

  1. ABiased coefficient estimates
  2. BUnreliable standard errors and invalid t-tests
  3. CA spurious unit root
  4. DPerfect multicollinearity
Show the answer

B. Unreliable standard errors and invalid t-tests

Coefficients remain unbiased and consistent, but the standard errors are wrong, so hypothesis tests based on them are invalid.

Curriculum area: Quantitative Methods — Model Misspecification, LOS 3.a

Question 10

A machine learning model shows very low training error and substantially higher validation error. This is best described as:

  1. AUnderfitting
  2. BOverfitting
  3. CData leakage
  4. DCorrect generalisation
Show the answer

B. Overfitting

A large gap between training and validation performance means the model has fitted noise in the training sample rather than signal.

Curriculum area: Quantitative Methods — Machine Learning, LOS 6.c

Economics

5–10% of the exam

Two dense modules: currency equilibrium value and economic growth.

Question 11

An investor borrows in a low-interest-rate currency and invests in a high-interest-rate currency. This carry trade is profitable only if:

  1. AUncovered interest rate parity holds
  2. BUncovered interest rate parity fails
  3. CCovered interest rate parity fails
  4. DPurchasing power parity holds in the short run
Show the answer

B. Uncovered interest rate parity fails

If uncovered parity held, the high-rate currency would depreciate by exactly the interest differential and the trade would earn nothing. Its empirical failure is the source of carry returns.

Curriculum area: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.d

Question 12

Under the neoclassical growth model, a permanent increase in the savings rate will:

  1. ARaise the long-run growth rate of output per capita
  2. BRaise the level of output per capita but not its long-run growth rate
  3. CHave no effect on either the level or the growth rate
  4. DLower the steady-state capital-to-labour ratio
Show the answer

B. Raise the level of output per capita but not its long-run growth rate

Diminishing returns to capital mean higher saving raises the steady-state level of output per worker but leaves the long-run growth rate set by technology.

Curriculum area: Economics — Economic Growth, LOS 2.d

Question 13

Relative purchasing power parity predicts that a country with persistently higher inflation than its trading partners will experience:

  1. ACurrency appreciation
  2. BCurrency depreciation
  3. CNo change in the nominal exchange rate
  4. DA widening real interest differential only
Show the answer

B. Currency depreciation

Higher relative inflation erodes purchasing power, so relative PPP predicts nominal depreciation roughly equal to the inflation differential.

Curriculum area: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.b

Question 14

Poorer countries that adopt the right institutional framework converge toward the income levels of richer members of their group, while countries without those institutions fall further behind. This is best described as:

  1. AAbsolute convergence
  2. BConditional convergence
  3. CClub convergence
  4. DDivergence
Show the answer

C. Club convergence

Club convergence: countries converge only within a club defined by institutional features, and those outside it can fall behind. Conditional convergence instead turns on shared savings rates, population growth and technology.

Curriculum area: Economics — Economic Growth, LOS 2.f

Question 15

Under the FX portfolio balance approach, a country running persistent fiscal deficits will most likely see its currency:

  1. AAppreciate indefinitely as capital flows in
  2. BAppreciate in the short run and depreciate in the long run
  3. CDepreciate immediately and permanently
  4. DRemain unchanged in both horizons
Show the answer

B. Appreciate in the short run and depreciate in the long run

Higher rates attract capital and support the currency initially, but investors eventually demand compensation for the growing debt burden, causing depreciation.

Curriculum area: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.g

Financial Statement Analysis

10–15% of the exam

Intercorporate investments, pensions, multinationals, report quality.

Question 16

An investor holds 30% of an investee and exercises significant influence. Compared with proportionate consolidation, the equity method will report:

  1. AHigher revenue and higher total assets
  2. BThe same net income but lower revenue and lower total assets
  3. CLower net income and higher total assets
  4. DThe same revenue but different net income
Show the answer

B. The same net income but lower revenue and lower total assets

The equity method reports a single line for both the investment and the share of income. Net income is identical; revenue and assets are not grossed up.

Curriculum area: FSA — Intercorporate Investments, LOS 1.c

Question 17

A sponsor lowers the discount rate used to value its defined benefit obligation. All else equal, this will:

  1. ADecrease the PBO and improve funded status
  2. BIncrease the PBO and worsen funded status
  3. CLeave the PBO unchanged
  4. DIncrease plan assets
Show the answer

B. Increase the PBO and worsen funded status

A lower discount rate raises the present value of future benefits, increasing the PBO and reducing the funded status.

Curriculum area: FSA — Employee Compensation: Post-Employment, LOS 2.c

Question 18

A subsidiary’s functional currency is its local currency. The appropriate translation method and the treatment of the resulting adjustment are:

  1. ATemporal method; gain or loss in net income
  2. BCurrent rate method; adjustment in other comprehensive income
  3. CTemporal method; adjustment in OCI
  4. DCurrent rate method; gain or loss in net income
Show the answer

B. Current rate method; adjustment in other comprehensive income

When the functional currency is the local currency, the current rate method applies and the cumulative translation adjustment goes to OCI, not income.

Curriculum area: FSA — Multinational Operations, LOS 3.b

Question 19

Which of the following is the strongest indicator of low reporting quality rather than merely low results quality?

  1. ADeclining margins across several periods
  2. BA large one-off restructuring charge
  3. CFrequent, unexplained changes in accounting estimates that raise reported income
  4. DA cyclical downturn in revenue
Show the answer

C. Frequent, unexplained changes in accounting estimates that raise reported income

Reporting quality concerns the faithfulness of the numbers. Repeated income-increasing estimate changes point to biased reporting, not just poor performance.

Curriculum area: FSA — Evaluating Quality of Financial Reports, LOS 5.b

Question 20

In analysing a bank, which ratio most directly addresses the adequacy of loss absorption capacity?

  1. ACommon equity tier 1 ratio
  2. BNet interest margin
  3. CCost-to-income ratio
  4. DLoan-to-deposit ratio
Show the answer

A. Common equity tier 1 ratio

CET1 measures the highest-quality capital against risk-weighted assets, which is precisely the loss-absorption question. The others address profitability or funding.

Curriculum area: FSA — Analysis of Financial Institutions, LOS 4.b

Corporate Finance

5–10% of the exam

Dividends and buybacks, ESG, advanced cost of capital, restructuring.

Question 21

A firm repurchases shares at a market price above book value per share. The effect on book value per share is:

  1. AAn increase
  2. BA decrease
  3. CNo change
  4. DAn increase only if funded with cash
Show the answer

B. A decrease

Repurchasing above book value removes more equity per share than the shares carried, so book value per share falls. Below book value it rises.

Curriculum area: Corporate Finance — Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases, LOS 1.d

Question 22

A firm earns EPS of $4.00, targets a 40% payout ratio and currently pays $1.00. With an adjustment factor of 0.25, the target payout adjustment model predicts a dividend increase of:

EPS $4.00 · target payout 40% · D₀ $1.00 · adj 0.25
  1. A$0.15
  2. B$0.25
  3. C$0.60
  4. D$1.60
Show the answer

A. $0.15

Expected increase = (4.00 × 0.40 − 1.00) × 0.25 = (1.60 − 1.00) × 0.25 = $0.15. The full gap is closed only gradually.

Curriculum area: Corporate Finance — Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases, LOS 1.f

Question 23

When estimating the beta of a private firm using the pure-play method, the analyst should relever the unlevered beta using:

  1. AThe comparable company's debt-to-equity ratio
  2. BThe subject company's target debt-to-equity ratio
  3. CThe industry average debt-to-equity ratio
  4. DA zero debt-to-equity ratio
Show the answer

B. The subject company's target debt-to-equity ratio

Unlever with the comparable's capital structure to strip it out, then relever with the subject firm's target structure. Using the comparable's in both steps is the planted error.

Curriculum area: Corporate Finance — Cost of Capital: Advanced Topics, LOS 3.c

Question 24

In a share-exchange acquisition rather than a cash acquisition, the risk that expected synergies fail to materialise is:

  1. ABorne entirely by the acquirer
  2. BShared between acquirer and target shareholders
  3. CBorne entirely by the target
  4. DEliminated by the exchange ratio
Show the answer

B. Shared between acquirer and target shareholders

Target shareholders become owners of the combined entity, so they share in any shortfall. In a cash deal the acquirer bears the full risk.

Curriculum area: Corporate Finance — Corporate Restructuring, LOS 4.d

Question 25

Which ESG integration approach explicitly excludes entire industries from the investable universe?

  1. ABest-in-class
  2. BNegative screening
  3. CThematic investing
  4. DEngagement and active ownership
Show the answer

B. Negative screening

Negative screening removes sectors or activities outright. Best-in-class instead selects leaders within every sector.

Curriculum area: Corporate Finance — Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) Considerations in Investment Analysis, LOS 2.b

Equities

10–15% of the exam

DDM, free cash flow, multiples, residual income, private companies.

Question 26

A firm has negative free cash flow to equity, pays no dividend, and applies clean surplus accounting. The most appropriate valuation model is:

  1. AGordon growth dividend discount model
  2. BFCFE discount model
  3. CResidual income model
  4. DTwo-stage dividend discount model
Show the answer

C. Residual income model

Residual income recognises much of the value in current book value, making it workable where dividends and near-term free cash flow are absent.

Curriculum area: Equity Valuation — Residual Income Valuation, LOS 5.a

Question 27

An analyst forecasts FCFF and discounts it at the cost of equity. This produces:

  1. AA correct estimate of equity value
  2. BA correct estimate of firm value
  3. CAn overstated value, because the discount rate is too high for the cash flow
  4. DAn understated value, because the discount rate is too high for the cash flow
Show the answer

D. An understated value, because the discount rate is too high for the cash flow

FCFF belongs to all capital providers and must be discounted at WACC. The cost of equity exceeds WACC, so discounting FCFF at it understates value.

Curriculum area: Equity Valuation — Free Cash Flow Valuation, LOS 3.b

Question 28

A firm has book value per share of $20, expected EPS of $3.00 and a required return on equity of 11%. The residual income for the coming year is:

B₀ = $20 · EPS $3.00 · r = 11%
  1. A$0.80
  2. B$2.20
  3. C$3.00
  4. D$5.20
Show the answer

A. $0.80

RI = EPS − r × B₀ = 3.00 − 0.11 × 20 = 3.00 − 2.20 = $0.80. The equity charge is what distinguishes RI from accounting earnings.

Curriculum area: Equity Valuation — Residual Income Valuation, LOS 5.c

Question 29

A controlling-interest value is $100 per share. A discount for lack of control of 20% and a discount for lack of marketability of 15% apply. The minority, non-marketable value is closest to:

Control value $100 · DLOC 20% · DLOM 15%
  1. A$65.00
  2. B$68.00
  3. C$70.00
  4. D$80.00
Show the answer

B. $68.00

100 × (1 − 0.20) × (1 − 0.15) = 100 × 0.80 × 0.85 = $68.00. The discounts compound; adding them to 35% is the planted shortcut.

Curriculum area: Equity Valuation — Private Company Valuation, LOS 6.e

Question 30

Compared with a trailing P/E, a justified leading P/E is preferable when:

  1. AEarnings are volatile and the most recent year was unrepresentative
  2. BThe firm has no analyst coverage
  3. CBook value is negative
  4. DThe firm pays no dividend
Show the answer

A. Earnings are volatile and the most recent year was unrepresentative

Leading P/E uses forecast normalised earnings, which avoids anchoring on an unrepresentative trailing figure.

Curriculum area: Equity Valuation — Market-Based Valuation, LOS 4.c

Fixed Income

10–15% of the exam

Term structure dynamics, arbitrage-free valuation, embedded options, CDS.

Question 31

A manager expects rates to evolve exactly as implied by the current forward curve. Riding the yield curve will most likely produce:

  1. AExcess return over a buy-and-hold strategy
  2. BThe same return as any other maturity strategy
  3. CA loss relative to the benchmark
  4. DA gain only if the curve is inverted
Show the answer

B. The same return as any other maturity strategy

If rates evolve as the forwards imply, all maturity strategies earn the same return by no-arbitrage. A view only pays if it differs from the forwards.

Curriculum area: Fixed Income — The Term Structure and Interest Rate Dynamics, LOS 1.d

Question 32

The one-year spot rate is 3% and the two-year spot rate is 4%. The one-year forward rate one year from now is closest to:

z₁ = 3% · z₂ = 4%
  1. A3.50%
  2. B4.00%
  3. C5.01%
  4. D7.00%
Show the answer

C. 5.01%

(1.04)² = (1.03)(1 + f), so 1 + f = 1.0816/1.03 = 1.0501 and f = 5.01%. Forward rates exceed spot rates on an upward-sloping curve.

Curriculum area: Fixed Income — The Term Structure and Interest Rate Dynamics, LOS 1.b

Question 33

Relative to an otherwise identical option-free bond, a callable bond has:

  1. AA higher value and higher effective duration
  2. BA lower value and lower effective duration when rates fall
  3. CA higher value and lower effective duration
  4. DThe same value but different convexity
Show the answer

B. A lower value and lower effective duration when rates fall

The call belongs to the issuer, so the bond is worth less. As rates fall, the call compresses price appreciation, shortening effective duration.

Curriculum area: Fixed Income — Valuation and Analysis of Bonds with Embedded Options, LOS 3.c

Question 34

Which criticism applies most directly to structural credit models such as Merton?

  1. AThey require an estimation sample of historical defaults
  2. BThey rely on unobservable asset values and volatilities
  3. CThey cannot incorporate the capital structure
  4. DThey assume default is a surprise jump process
Show the answer

B. They rely on unobservable asset values and volatilities

Structural models require the firm's asset value and asset volatility, neither of which is directly observable. Reduced-form models face the estimation-sample criticism.

Curriculum area: Fixed Income — Credit Analysis Models, LOS 4.d

Question 35

A credit protection buyer on a CDS is economically equivalent to:

  1. ABeing long the reference bond
  2. BBeing short credit risk of the reference entity
  3. CSelling a put on the reference entity
  4. DBeing long the benchmark government bond only
Show the answer

B. Being short credit risk of the reference entity

Buying protection pays off when credit deteriorates, so it is a short credit position — the mirror image of owning the bond.

Curriculum area: Fixed Income — Credit Default Swaps, LOS 5.b

Derivatives

5–10% of the exam

Forward commitments and contingent claims — binomial, BSM, the Greeks.

Question 36

Three months after inception, the forward price on a contract has risen from $50 to $53. With six months remaining and a risk-free rate of 4%, the value to the long is closest to:

F₀ = $50 · Fₜ = $53 · T − t = 0.5 · r = 4%
  1. A$2.94
  2. B$3.00
  3. C$3.06
  4. D$0.00
Show the answer

A. $2.94

V = (Fₜ − F₀)/(1 + r)^(T−t) = 3/(1.04)^0.5 = 3/1.0198 = $2.94. The value is the discounted difference between forward prices.

Curriculum area: Derivatives — Pricing and Valuation of Forward Commitments, LOS 1.c

Question 37

In the Black–Scholes–Merton model, N(d₂) is best interpreted as:

  1. AThe delta of the call
  2. BThe risk-neutral probability the option finishes in the money
  3. CThe actual probability the option finishes in the money
  4. DThe present value factor applied to the strike
Show the answer

B. The risk-neutral probability the option finishes in the money

N(d₂) is the risk-neutral probability of exercise. N(d₁) is the option's delta, and the actual probability is not used in the model at all.

Curriculum area: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.d

Question 38

A dealer is short gamma and rebalances a delta hedge only weekly. A large move in the underlying in either direction will most likely produce:

  1. AA hedging gain
  2. BA hedging loss
  3. CNo effect, because the book is delta neutral
  4. DA gain only if the move is upward
Show the answer

B. A hedging loss

A short gamma position loses on large moves in either direction because the delta hedge is systematically wrong between rebalancing dates.

Curriculum area: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.h

Question 39

An increase in the volatility of the underlying will, all else equal:

  1. AIncrease call values and decrease put values
  2. BDecrease call values and increase put values
  3. CIncrease both call and put values
  4. DLeave both unchanged
Show the answer

C. Increase both call and put values

Higher volatility raises the value of optionality on both sides, because the downside is truncated by the option structure while the upside is not.

Curriculum area: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.f

Question 40

Two months after inception of a plain vanilla interest rate swap, market rates have fallen. The value of the swap to the fixed-rate payer is:

  1. APositive
  2. BNegative
  3. CZero
  4. DPositive only if the swap is collateralised
Show the answer

B. Negative

The fixed payer is locked into paying an above-market rate while receiving less floating. The swap has negative value to that side.

Curriculum area: Derivatives — Pricing and Valuation of Forward Commitments, LOS 1.f

Alternative Investments

5–10% of the exam

Commodities, real estate direct and listed, hedge fund strategies.

Question 41

A commodity futures curve is in contango. An investor holding a long-only rolling futures position will most likely earn:

  1. AA positive roll return
  2. BA negative roll return
  3. CA roll return of exactly zero
  4. DA roll return independent of the curve shape
Show the answer

B. A negative roll return

In contango the investor sells a cheaper expiring contract and buys a more expensive deferred one, producing a negative roll return.

Curriculum area: Alternative Investments — Introduction to Commodities, LOS 1.d

Question 42

A REIT reports net income of $40m, depreciation of $22m and gains on property sales of $6m. Funds from operations is:

NI $40m · Dep $22m · Gains $6m
  1. A$50m
  2. B$56m
  3. C$62m
  4. D$68m
Show the answer

B. $56m

FFO = NI + depreciation − gains on sales = 40 + 22 − 6 = $56m. Gains are excluded because they are not recurring operating income.

Curriculum area: Alternative Investments — Investments in Real Estate through Publicly Traded Securities, LOS 3.c

Question 43

A merger arbitrage strategy has a return profile most similar to:

  1. AA long call on the target
  2. BA short put on deal completion
  3. CA long straddle
  4. DA long position in the acquirer
Show the answer

B. A short put on deal completion

The strategy earns a small steady spread when deals close and suffers large losses when they break — the payoff shape of a short put.

Curriculum area: Alternative Investments — Hedge Fund Strategies, LOS 4.c

Question 44

Compared with direct real estate ownership, publicly traded REITs most likely exhibit:

  1. ALower liquidity and lower short-run equity correlation
  2. BHigher liquidity and higher short-run equity correlation
  3. CHigher liquidity and lower short-run equity correlation
  4. DIdentical liquidity and correlation
Show the answer

B. Higher liquidity and higher short-run equity correlation

Listed vehicles trade continuously, which gives liquidity but also imports short-run equity market beta that direct property does not display.

Curriculum area: Alternative Investments — Investments in Real Estate through Publicly Traded Securities, LOS 3.a

Question 45

Under the theory of storage, a market in contango is best explained by:

  1. AA high convenience yield
  2. BHigh storage costs relative to the convenience yield
  3. CProducer hedging pressure
  4. DBackwardated inventory levels
Show the answer

B. High storage costs relative to the convenience yield

Contango arises when the cost of carrying inventory exceeds the convenience yield of holding the physical commodity.

Curriculum area: Alternative Investments — Introduction to Commodities, LOS 1.e

Portfolio Construction

10–15% of the exam

Active management, ETFs, multifactor models, market risk, backtesting.

Question 46

A manager has an information coefficient of 0.05, 100 independent bets per year and a transfer coefficient of 0.8. The expected information ratio is closest to:

IC 0.05 · BR 100 · TC 0.8
  1. A0.04
  2. B0.40
  3. C0.50
  4. D4.00
Show the answer

B. 0.40

IR ≈ IC × √BR × TC = 0.05 × 10 × 0.8 = 0.40. Breadth enters as a square root, so doubling bets does not double the ratio.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management — Analysis of Active Portfolio Management, LOS 2.d

Question 47

A portfolio holds 400 stocks but all positions reflect a single macroeconomic view. Under the fundamental law of active management, breadth is closest to:

  1. A400
  2. B20
  3. C1
  4. D400 divided by the transfer coefficient
Show the answer

C. 1

Breadth counts independent decisions, not holdings. One macro view driving every position is a breadth of one.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management — Analysis of Active Portfolio Management, LOS 2.e

Question 48

A portfolio has a one-day 95% VaR of $1.2m. The most accurate interpretation is:

VaR₉₅,₁d = $1.2m
  1. AThe maximum loss on any single day is $1.2m
  2. BOn 5% of days the loss is expected to exceed $1.2m
  3. CThe average daily loss is $1.2m
  4. DLosses will never exceed $1.2m in a month
Show the answer

B. On 5% of days the loss is expected to exceed $1.2m

VaR is a quantile of the loss distribution. It states the frequency with which the threshold is breached, never the size of the breach.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management — Measuring and Managing Market Risk, LOS 5.b

Question 49

A backtest uses financial statement data as later restated rather than as originally reported. This introduces:

  1. ASurvivorship bias
  2. BLook-ahead bias
  3. CData snooping bias
  4. DSelection bias
Show the answer

B. Look-ahead bias

Restated data was not available at the decision date, so the backtest uses information the manager could not have had — look-ahead bias.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management — Backtesting and Simulation, LOS 6.c

Question 50

An ETF trades persistently at a premium to net asset value. The most likely cause is:

  1. AA failure of the creation/redemption mechanism
  2. BHigh portfolio turnover within the fund
  3. CA high expense ratio
  4. DA large number of authorised participants
Show the answer

A. A failure of the creation/redemption mechanism

Arbitrage by authorised participants normally closes premiums and discounts. A persistent premium implies the mechanism is impaired.

Curriculum area: Portfolio Management — Exchange-Traded Funds, LOS 3.c
