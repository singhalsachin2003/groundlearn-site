Credit Suisse AT1 bonds, 2023

Contingent capital and contract (trigger) risk
Bondholders wiped out while shareholders were paid.

At a glance

When19 March 2023
WhoCredit Suisse, its takeover by UBS, and holders of its AT1 bonds
InstrumentsAdditional tier 1 (AT1) contingent convertible bonds
LossCHF 16bn of AT1 bonds written down to zero
Risk typeContingent capital and contract (trigger) risk

What happened

After years of losses and scandals, including about $5.5bn on Archegos, Credit Suisse lost the confidence of clients and markets as US regional banks failed in March 2023. Over the weekend of 18 and 19 March the Swiss authorities arranged its takeover by UBS, backed by government guarantees and central bank liquidity. The regulator, FINMA, ordered CHF 16bn of Credit Suisse's AT1 bonds written down to zero, while shareholders received UBS shares worth about CHF 0.76 each.

Why it went wrong

Most investors treated AT1 bonds as ranking above shareholders, because that is the order in a bankruptcy or a standard resolution. But this was a government-supported takeover, and Credit Suisse's AT1 terms included a viability-event trigger: the bonds could be written off if the bank received extraordinary public support, whatever its capital ratio. Swiss AT1 in this form was written down rather than converted into shares, so holders received nothing.

What it teaches

Read the trigger language, not the asset class label. A bond's ranking depends on its own terms and governing law as well as the general creditor hierarchy, and jurisdictions differ: EU and UK authorities said the next day that in their systems shareholders absorb losses first. The trigger that matters may be a regulator's judgment of viability rather than a published ratio.

Key terms

Additional tier 1
Perpetual bank capital with cancellable coupons, designed to absorb losses before senior creditors.
Contingent convertible
A bond that converts into equity or is written down when a specified trigger is hit.
Viability event
A trigger based on a regulator’s judgment that a bank cannot survive without extraordinary support.
Write-down
Reducing a bond’s principal, in part or in full, without giving holders anything in exchange.
CET1 ratio
Common equity tier 1 capital as a share of risk-weighted assets, the core measure of bank solvency.
Creditor hierarchy
The order in which claims on a failed firm absorb losses, normally equity first.
