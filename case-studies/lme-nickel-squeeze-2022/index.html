The LME nickel squeeze, 2022

Basis risk, margin calls and market closure
A hedge that could not be delivered into.

At a glance

When7 to 8 March 2022
WhoTsingshan, a Chinese nickel producer, and the London Metal Exchange
InstrumentsLME nickel futures and bilateral short positions with banks
LossAbout $3.9bn of trades cancelled; a standstill with banks
Risk typeBasis risk, margin calls and market closure

What happened

Nickel was already rising in early 2022 when Russia's invasion of Ukraine added a supply shock. On 7 and 8 March the LME price roughly quadrupled, briefly topping $100,000 a tonne, as holders of short positions were forced to buy back into a thin market. The largest short belonged to Tsingshan, a producer hedging its own output. The LME halted trading on the morning of 8 March, cancelled about $3.9bn of trades done that morning, and did not reopen the contract until 16 March.

Why it went wrong

A producer selling forward against its output is the most standard hedge there is, but Tsingshan mainly produced nickel pig iron and matte, which cannot be delivered against the LME contract. The hedge followed a price it could not settle with its own metal. Margin on the futures was due in cash every day, while the higher value of unsold metal brought in no cash at all, so calls forced buying into the squeeze and pushed the price higher still.

What it teaches

Check what a contract actually delivers, not just what it tracks: a hedge in a grade you do not produce is a basis position. Size the cash you will need to fund a hedge daily, not just whether it works at maturity. And remember that exchanges can halt trading and even cancel trades: a market being open is an assumption. The High Court later upheld the cancellation as lawful.

Key terms

Short squeeze
A rise driven by shorts being forced to buy back into a market with too little offer to absorb them.
Deliverable grade
The specification a contract can be settled with physically — and the thing a producer hedge must match.
Basis risk
The exposure left when a hedge references something other than the asset actually held.
Variation margin
The daily cash settlement of a derivative’s mark-to-market, owed whatever the physical position is worth.
Trade cancellation
An exchange’s power to void executed trades, used here for a whole session’s nickel business.
Liquidity risk
The risk of being unable to fund a position that is economically sound, which is what closes most of them.
