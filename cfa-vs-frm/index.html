CFA vs FRM: which exam fits?

The two programmes side by side, from the official sources, and how to choose between them.

Side by side

Which one fits

The CFA Program suits people heading for investment roles: equity and credit research, portfolio management, wealth management, investment banking and consulting. It is broad, long and heavy on valuation and financial statements.

The FRM suits people in or heading for risk roles: market and credit risk, model validation, treasury and ALM, regulatory and risk consulting. It is narrower and deeper on risk measurement, and shorter to complete.

Plenty of people hold both. The overlap is real but partial: quantitative methods, fixed income, derivatives and portfolio risk appear in both, so studying one gives you a head start on the other, but each has large areas the other barely touches.

Questions people ask

Is the FRM easier than the CFA?

It is shorter: two exams rather than three. Whether it is easier depends on your background. Candidates with strong maths often find FRM Part I familiar, while CFA Level I covers more ground, with more accounting.

Can I take both?

Yes. Many candidates finish one and start the other. If you plan to, take the one closer to your current or target role first.

Which is better for a career in risk?

The FRM is built for risk work and is widely recognised by banks and regulators. The CFA charter is also valued in risk roles, especially those close to investment decisions.
