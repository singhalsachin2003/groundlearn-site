A

Accrual accountingCFA
Recognising revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, regardless of when cash moves.
Active shareCFA
The proportion of a portfolio’s holdings that differs from its benchmark, from 0% for an index fund to 100% for no overlap.
Adverse selectionCFAFRM
The problem that arises when the party with better information self-selects into a transaction, leaving the counterparty with the worse…
Agency problemCFAFRM
The conflict that arises when managers act in their own interest rather than that of the owners who employ them.
AlphaCFA
Return in excess of what the asset’s risk exposures would predict.
AnnuityCFA
A series of equal cash flows at equal intervals.
Arbitrage pricing theoryCFAFRM
A model deriving expected returns from exposures to multiple systematic factors, assuming no arbitrage rather than a single market…
Arithmetic meanCFAFRM
The sum of the observations divided by their number.
Asset locationCFA
Deciding which account type holds which asset, so that heavily taxed returns sit in tax-sheltered accounts.

B

BacktestingFRM
Comparing realised losses with the VaR forecasts that preceded them, to check that breaches occur about as often as the confidence level…
Balance sheetCFA
A statement of what a company owns and owes at a single point in time.
Bank runFRM
A self-fulfilling withdrawal of funding from an institution, where the rational individual choice to withdraw first makes failure certain…
Basel AccordsFRM
The international framework for bank capital and liquidity regulation, issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision.
Basis riskCFAFRM
The risk that a hedge and the exposure it hedges do not move together, leaving a residual.
Bayes’ formulaCFA
A rule for revising the probability of an event in the light of new information, using how likely that information is if the event is or…
Behavioural biasCFA
A systematic departure from rational decision-making, either cognitive — an error of reasoning that education can correct — or emotional,…
BetaCFAFRM
The sensitivity of an asset’s return to the market’s return — its share of systematic risk.
Black–Scholes–Merton modelCFAFRM
A closed-form model for European option prices, assuming lognormal prices, constant volatility and continuous frictionless trading.
Book valueCFA
The accounting value of equity: assets less liabilities as reported.
BootstrapCFAFRM
Resampling the observed data with replacement to estimate the sampling distribution of a statistic, without assuming a parametric form.
Bootstrapping (yield curve)CFAFRM
Deriving spot rates sequentially from the prices of coupon bonds, using each solved rate to strip the next.
Business cycleCFA
The recurring pattern of expansion, peak, contraction and trough in economic activity.
Business riskCFAFRM
The uncertainty in operating income arising from the nature of the business itself, before any financing decision.

C

Call optionCFAFRM
The right, without the obligation, to buy an asset at the strike price by expiry.
Callable bondCFA
A bond the issuer may redeem early, usually when rates have fallen.
Capital allocation lineCFA
The set of risk–return combinations available by mixing a risky portfolio with the risk-free asset.
Capital asset pricing modelCFAFRM
A model stating that an asset’s expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium.
Capital budgetingCFA
The process of deciding which long-term investments a company should make.
Capital callCFA
The general partner’s demand that limited partners transfer part of their committed capital.
Capital market lineCFA
The capital allocation line that runs from the risk-free asset to the market portfolio, the steepest available.
Capital structureCFA
The mix of debt and equity a company uses to fund itself.
Capitalisation rateCFA
Net operating income divided by property value — a property’s unlevered income yield.
Carried interestCFA
The general partner’s share of a private fund’s profits, typically around 20%.
Carry tradeCFAFRM
Borrowing in a low interest rate currency to invest in a high interest rate one, profiting if the exchange rate does not move against you.
Cash conversion cycleCFA
The time between paying for inventory and collecting cash from the customer.
Cash flow additivityCFA
The principle that the value of a set of cash flows equals the sum of the values of its parts, so two ways of reaching the same future…
Cash flow from operationsCFA
Cash generated by the core business, before investment and financing.
Central counterpartyFRM
An entity that interposes itself between the two sides of a trade, becoming buyer to the seller and seller to the buyer.
Central limit theoremCFAFRM
For a large enough sample, the distribution of the sample mean approaches normal regardless of the shape of the underlying population.
Code of EthicsCFA
The aspirational statement of how members and candidates should conduct themselves.
Coefficient of determinationCFAFRM
The fraction of the dependent variable’s variation explained by the regression.
Coherent risk measureFRM
A risk measure satisfying monotonicity, subadditivity, positive homogeneity and translation invariance.
CollarCFA
Holding the underlying, buying a put and writing a call, bounding the outcome between a floor and a cap.
CollateralFRM
Assets pledged to secure an exposure, reducing loss if the counterparty defaults.
Common shareCFA
An ownership claim on a company’s residual cash flows and assets, normally carrying a vote and ranking last in liquidation.
CompositeCFA
All portfolios managed to the same strategy or objective, grouped together for GIPS reporting.
Confidence intervalCFAFRM
A range that will contain the true parameter in a stated proportion of repeated samples.
Contango and backwardationCFAFRM
Contango is a futures price above the spot price; backwardation is a futures price below it.
Continuous compoundingCFAFRM
Compounding over infinitely small intervals.
ConvexityCFAFRM
The curvature of the price–yield relationship — how duration itself changes as yields move.
Core inflationCFA
Inflation excluding food and energy prices, which are volatile and often driven by supply rather than demand.
Corporate governanceCFA
The system of controls by which a company is directed and held accountable to its stakeholders.
CorrelationCFAFRM
Covariance divided by the product of the two standard deviations, giving a unit-free number between −1 and +1.
Cost of equityCFA
The return equity holders require for bearing the residual risk of the business.
Counterparty credit riskFRM
The risk that the other side of a derivative defaults before settling, when the contract has positive value to you.
Coupon rateCFA
The annual interest a bond pays, as a percentage of par.
CovarianceCFAFRM
A measure of how two variables move together.
Covered callCFA
Holding the underlying and writing a call against it, collecting the premium in exchange for capping the upside.
Credit default swapCFAFRM
A contract in which the buyer pays a periodic premium and receives compensation if a specified borrower suffers a credit event.
Credit spreadCFAFRM
The extra yield a borrower pays over a risk-free benchmark of the same maturity, compensating for default and liquidity risk.
Credit valuation adjustmentFRM
The reduction in a derivative’s value to reflect the possibility that the counterparty defaults.
Currency hedgingCFA
Using forwards, futures or options to reduce a portfolio’s exposure to exchange rate movements on foreign assets.
Current ratioCFA
Current assets divided by current liabilities, a rough test of short-term solvency.
Current yieldCFA
A bond’s annual coupon divided by its current market price — the cash income it throws off this year per unit invested.

D

Data miningCFAFRM
Searching a dataset repeatedly until a relationship appears, then presenting it as though it had been hypothesised in advance.
Deferred taxCFA
Tax arising from timing differences between accounting profit and taxable profit, recognised as an asset or a liability.
DeltaCFAFRM
The change in an option’s value for a small change in the underlying price.
Delta hedgingCFAFRM
Offsetting an option position with the underlying so the combined position is insensitive to small price moves.
DepreciationCFA
The systematic allocation of a tangible asset’s cost over its useful life.
DerivativeCFAFRM
A contract whose value depends on the price of something else — an asset, a rate, an index or an event.
Derivatives overlayCFA
Adjusting a portfolio’s exposures with derivatives while leaving the underlying holdings untouched.
Diligence and reasonable basisCFA
The requirement that any recommendation or action rest on adequate research and analysis that could be defended if challenged.
Distribution waterfallCFA
The agreed order in which a private fund’s proceeds are shared between limited and general partners.
DiversificationCFAFRM
Combining imperfectly correlated assets so that portfolio risk falls below the weighted average of the individual risks.
Dividend discount modelCFA
Valuing a share as the present value of its expected future dividends.
Dividend policyCFA
How a company decides to return cash to shareholders, and in what form.
DuPont analysisCFA
Decomposing ROE into its drivers — profitability, efficiency and leverage — to identify what is actually moving it.
DurationCFAFRM
The sensitivity of a bond’s price to a change in yield, and equivalently the weighted average time to receiving its cash flows.
DV01CFAFRM
The change in a position’s value for a one basis point change in yield, expressed in currency.

E

Earnings per shareCFA
Net income available to common shareholders, divided by the weighted average shares outstanding.
Earnings qualityCFA
The degree to which reported earnings are sustainable and backed by cash rather than by estimates and one-offs.
EBITDACFA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a rough proxy for operating cash generation.
Economic capitalFRM
The capital a firm judges it needs to absorb unexpected losses at a chosen confidence level, computed internally.
Effective annual rateCFA
The rate that, compounded once a year, gives the same growth as a stated nominal rate compounded more often.
Effective durationCFAFRM
Duration measured by repricing the bond under small parallel shifts in the benchmark curve, so that changes in cash flows are captured.
Efficient frontierCFA
The set of portfolios offering the highest expected return for each level of risk.
Enterprise valueCFA
The value of the whole business regardless of how it is financed: market capitalisation plus debt and minorities, less cash.
Expected lossFRM
The average credit loss anticipated over a horizon.
Expected shortfallFRM
The average loss in the cases where the VaR threshold is breached — the expected size of a bad day, given that it is a bad day.
Exposure at defaultFRM
The amount outstanding when default occurs.

F

Factor investingCFAFRM
Building portfolios around systematic return drivers — value, size, momentum, quality, low volatility — rather than around individual…
Fair dealingCFA
Treating all clients fairly when disseminating recommendations or taking investment action.
Fiduciary dutyCFA
The obligation to act in another party’s interest ahead of your own, with the care and loyalty that relationship demands.
Financial leverageCFA
The use of debt to finance assets, magnifying returns to equity in both directions.
Fiscal policyCFA
Government use of spending and taxation to influence aggregate demand.
Forward contractCFAFRM
A private agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date.
Forward rateCFAFRM
The interest rate agreed today for borrowing or lending over a future period, implied by today’s spot curve.
Free cash flowCFA
Cash available after the investment needed to maintain the business.
Free floatCFA
The portion of a company’s shares actually available to public investors, excluding strategic and locked-up holdings.
Front-runningCFA
Trading ahead of a client order to benefit from the price move that order will cause.
Fundamental law of active managementCFA
Expected active return is a function of skill, the number of independent decisions taken, and how fully those decisions are expressed in…
Funding liquidity riskFRM
The risk of being unable to meet obligations as they fall due without incurring unacceptable losses.
Future valueCFA
What a sum invested today grows to by a stated date at a stated rate.
Futures contractCFAFRM
A standardised, exchange-traded forward, marked to market daily through a clearing house.

G

GammaCFAFRM
The rate at which delta changes as the underlying moves.
General partnerCFA
The manager of a private fund, responsible for investment decisions and bearing unlimited liability for the partnership.
Geometric meanCFAFRM
The constant rate that would produce the observed compound growth over the whole period.
GIPSCFA
Voluntary standards for calculating and presenting investment performance, designed so that a prospective client can compare firms on a…
Goals-based investingCFA
Structuring wealth into separate portfolios for separate objectives, each with its own horizon and risk tolerance, rather than one…
GoodwillCFA
The excess of a purchase price over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired.
Gross domestic productCFA
The market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a period.

H

Harmonic meanCFA
The reciprocal of the average of reciprocals.
Hedge fundCFA
A privately offered pooled vehicle with broad freedom over strategy, leverage and short selling.
Hedge fund replicationFRM
Reproducing hedge fund returns using liquid instruments and factor exposures, rather than by paying for the funds themselves.
HeteroskedasticityCFAFRM
Regression errors whose variance is not constant across observations.
High-water markCFA
The highest value an investor’s holding has previously reached, above which incentive fees are charged.
Historical simulationCFAFRM
Estimating VaR by revaluing the current portfolio using actual historical changes in risk factors.
Human capitalCFA
The present value of an individual’s expected future earnings, treated as an asset alongside their financial wealth.
Hurdle rateCFA
The minimum return a fund must deliver before the manager earns an incentive fee.

I

Illiquidity premiumCFAFRM
The extra expected return demanded for holding an asset that cannot be sold quickly at a fair price.
ImmunisationCFA
Structuring a bond portfolio so that price and reinvestment effects cancel, locking in a return over a horizon.
Implementation shortfallCFA
The difference between the return on a paper portfolio and the return actually achieved, capturing delay, market impact, commissions and…
Implied volatilityCFAFRM
The volatility that, put into an option pricing model, returns the option’s observed market price.
Income statementCFA
A statement of revenue, expenses and profit over a period.
Independence and objectivityCFA
The requirement to use reasonable care and judgement to keep independence, and to refuse any gift or pressure that could compromise it.
Index weightingCFA
The rule that decides each member’s influence on an index: by share price, equally, by market capitalisation (optionally counting only…
InflationCFAFRM
A sustained rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money.
Information ratioCFA
Active return divided by tracking error — value added per unit of benchmark-relative risk.
Initial and variation marginCFAFRM
Initial margin is the good-faith deposit required to open a futures position; variation margin is the daily cash transfer that settles the…
Interest coverage ratioCFAFRM
EBIT divided by interest expense — how many times earnings cover the interest bill.
Interest rate parityCFAFRM
The condition that the forward exchange rate differs from the spot rate by exactly the interest rate differential, so hedged returns are…
Internal rate of returnCFA
The discount rate at which a project’s NPV is zero.
Investment policy statementCFA
The written agreement setting out an investor’s objectives, constraints and the rules the portfolio will be run by.

J

J-curveCFA
The typical shape of a private fund’s returns: negative early, as fees are paid before value is realised, then rising.

K

Key rate durationCFAFRM
Sensitivity to a change in one segment of the yield curve, holding the other segments fixed.
KurtosisCFAFRM
The weight of a distribution’s tails.

L

Leverage ratioFRM
Capital divided by total exposure, without risk weighting — a backstop to the risk-weighted requirement.
Leveraged buyoutCFA
Acquiring a company using a large proportion of borrowed money, secured against the target’s own assets and cash flows.
Liability-driven investingCFA
Managing a portfolio against the liabilities it must fund rather than against a market index.
LIFO and FIFOCFA
Inventory cost-flow assumptions.
Linear regressionCFAFRM
Fitting a straight line through data by minimising the sum of squared residuals, to explain a dependent variable by one or more…
Liquidity coverage ratioFRM
The requirement that a bank holds enough high-quality liquid assets to survive a thirty-day stress scenario.
Liquidity-adjusted VaRFRM
VaR extended to include the cost of closing the position — the bid–ask spread and the market impact of exiting at size.
Lock-up periodCFAFRM
The period during which an investor may not redeem from a fund.
Lognormal distributionCFAFRM
The distribution of a variable whose natural logarithm is normally distributed.
Loss given defaultFRM
The proportion of exposure lost when a borrower defaults, after recoveries.
Loyalty to employerCFA
The duty to act for the employer’s benefit, not to deprive it of your skills, and not to divulge confidential information.

M

Margin (equity trading)CFAFRM
Borrowing from a broker to buy securities, using the securities as collateral.
Marginal VaRFRM
The change in portfolio VaR from a small increase in one position — the position’s contribution to total risk at the margin.
Market capitalisationCFA
Share price multiplied by shares outstanding — the market value of a company’s equity.
Market efficiencyCFA
The proposition that prices already reflect available information, so consistently beating the market on that information is not possible.
Market impactCFAFRM
The adverse price move caused by the act of trading itself.
Market liquidity riskFRM
The risk that a position cannot be sold quickly without moving the price against you.
Market manipulationCFA
Transactions or information intended to distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume, in order to mislead other participants.
Market-capitalisation-weighted indexCFA
An index in which weights are proportional to constituents’ market values.
Material non-public informationCFA
Information that has not been released to the market and that a reasonable investor would want before trading.
Minimum-variance portfolioCFA
The combination of risky assets with the lowest possible variance.
Model riskFRM
The risk of loss from using a model that is wrong, or right but used outside the conditions it was built for.
Modified durationCFAFRM
The approximate percentage change in a bond’s price for a one percentage point change in yield.
Monetary policyCFAFRM
A central bank’s management of money supply and interest rates to pursue price stability and, in some mandates, employment.
Money-weighted returnCFA
The IRR of a portfolio including the timing and size of every contribution and withdrawal.
MoneynessCFA
Whether exercising an option now would pay: in-the-money, at-the-money or out-of-the-money.
Monte Carlo simulationCFAFRM
Estimating a distribution of outcomes by repeatedly drawing random inputs from assumed distributions and recomputing the result.
Moral hazardCFAFRM
The incentive to take more risk once someone else bears the consequences.
Mosaic theoryCFA
Combining public information with non-material non-public information to reach a material conclusion.
MulticollinearityCFAFRM
Two or more independent variables in a regression that are highly correlated.
Multifactor modelCFAFRM
A model explaining returns by exposure to several systematic factors rather than to the market alone.

N

Net present valueCFA
The present value of a project’s cash inflows less the present value of its outflows.
NettingFRM
Offsetting positive and negative exposures with the same counterparty so that only the net amount is at risk.
Neutral rateCFA
The real policy rate that neither stimulates nor restrains the economy when output is at potential.
Normal distributionCFAFRM
A symmetric, bell-shaped distribution fully described by its mean and variance.
Notional principalCFAFRM
The reference amount used to compute a derivative’s payments, which is usually never exchanged.
Null hypothesisCFAFRM
The statement a test assumes true until the data give sufficient reason to reject it.

O

Off-balance-sheet financingCFA
Arrangements that give a company the use of an asset or the benefit of borrowing without showing the liability on its balance sheet.
Operating leverageCFA
The sensitivity of operating income to a change in sales, driven by the proportion of fixed costs.
Operational riskFRM
The risk of loss from failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events.
Opportunity costCFA
The value of the best alternative given up by choosing one course of action.
Option-adjusted spreadCFAFRM
The spread over the benchmark curve once the value of any embedded option has been stripped out, making bonds with and without options…
Output gapCFA
The difference between actual and potential output, as a percentage of potential.
OverfittingCFA
A model that fits the noise in its training data as well as the signal, so it performs well in-sample and poorly on new data.

P

p-valueCFAFRM
The smallest significance level at which the null could be rejected — the probability of a result at least this extreme if the null were…
Par valueCFA
The amount a bond repays at maturity, and the base on which the coupon is calculated.
Parametric VaRFRM
Estimating VaR from an assumed distribution, usually normal, using the portfolio’s volatility and correlations.
Pecking order theoryCFA
The observation that firms prefer internal funds first, then debt, and issue equity last.
Performance attributionCFAFRM
Decomposing a portfolio’s return relative to its benchmark into the decisions that produced it — typically allocation, selection and…
PerpetuityCFA
An annuity with no end date — a level cash flow received forever.
Preference shareCFA
A share with a stated dividend that ranks ahead of common equity but behind all debt, usually without a vote.
Prepayment riskCFAFRM
The risk that borrowers repay mortgage principal early, most often when rates fall and refinancing becomes attractive.
Present valueCFA
What a future cash flow is worth today, found by discounting it at a rate that reflects its risk and timing.
Price elasticity of demandCFA
The percentage change in quantity demanded for a one percent change in price.
Price-to-earnings ratioCFA
Share price divided by earnings per share.
Price-weighted indexCFA
An index in which each constituent’s weight is proportional to its share price.
Private creditCFAFRM
Lending to companies outside the public bond and syndicated loan markets, usually at floating rates with bespoke covenants.
Private equityCFA
Equity investment in companies that are not publicly traded, typically through a closed-end fund with a fixed life.
Probability of defaultFRM
The likelihood that a borrower fails to meet its obligations over a given horizon.
ProcyclicalityFRM
The tendency of risk measures and capital requirements to loosen in booms and tighten in busts, amplifying the cycle.
Protective putCFA
Holding the underlying and buying a put, which sets a floor under the position at the cost of the premium.
Purchasing power parityCFA
The proposition that exchange rates adjust so that identical goods cost the same across countries.
Put optionCFAFRM
The right, without the obligation, to sell an asset at the strike price by expiry.
Putable bondCFA
A bond the holder may sell back to the issuer early, usually when rates have risen.
Put–call parityCFAFRM
The no-arbitrage relationship linking a European call, a European put, the underlying and a risk-free bond of the same strike and expiry.

R

RAROCFRM
Risk-adjusted return divided by economic capital, used to compare business lines on a like-for-like basis and to price risk.
Real versus nominalCFA
A nominal figure is measured in current prices; a real figure has been adjusted for inflation and so measures actual volume or purchasing…
RebalancingCFA
Trading back to target weights after market moves have shifted them.
Record retentionCFA
The requirement to keep the records supporting analysis, recommendations and actions.
Reinvestment riskCFA
The risk that coupons and principal must be reinvested at a lower rate than expected.
REITCFA
A listed vehicle holding income-producing property, generally required to distribute most of its income to keep favourable tax treatment.
Required rate of returnCFA
The minimum return an investor demands for holding an asset, given its risk.
Return on equityCFA
Net income as a percentage of average shareholders’ equity.
Return smoothingCFAFRM
Understated volatility caused by infrequent or appraisal-based valuations rather than by genuinely stable prices.
RhoCFAFRM
The sensitivity of an option’s value to a change in the risk-free rate.
Risk appetiteFRM
The amount and type of risk a firm is willing to take in pursuit of its objectives, set by the board and cascaded into limits.
Risk budgetingCFAFRM
Allocating a portfolio’s total risk across strategies or managers, rather than allocating its capital.
Risk toleranceCFA
How much risk an investor is both able and willing to take.
Rogue tradingFRM
Unauthorised positions concealed by an employee, typically enabled by weak separation between the front and back office.
Roll yieldCFA
The return from rolling a futures position forward as contracts expire, positive in backwardation and negative in contango.

S

SecuritisationCFAFRM
Pooling loans and issuing securities backed by their cash flows, usually through a bankruptcy-remote special purpose vehicle.
Serial correlationCFAFRM
Regression errors correlated with their own past values.
Share repurchaseCFA
A company buying its own shares, reducing the share count.
Sharpe ratioCFAFRM
Excess return over the risk-free rate, per unit of total risk.
Short sellingCFAFRM
Selling borrowed securities in the expectation of buying them back cheaper.
SkewnessCFAFRM
The asymmetry of a distribution.
Special situationsCFA
Investing around a specific corporate event — restructuring, distress, spin-off or litigation — where the outcome depends more on the…
Spot rateCFAFRM
The yield on a single cash flow received at one future date, with no intervening coupons.
StakeholderCFA
Any party with an interest in the company — shareholders, creditors, employees, customers, suppliers, regulators and the wider community.
Standard deviationCFAFRM
The square root of variance, in the same units as the data.
Standard errorCFAFRM
The standard deviation of a sample statistic, usually of the sample mean.
Standards of Professional ConductCFA
The seven enforceable standards covering professionalism, integrity of capital markets, duties to clients, duties to employers, investment…
Statement of cash flowsCFA
A statement reconciling opening and closing cash, split into operating, investing and financing activities.
StraddleCFA
Buying a call and a put at the same strike and expiry — a position on the size of a move rather than its direction.
Strategic asset allocationCFA
The long-run target mix of asset classes, set from the investor’s objectives and constraints.
Stress testingFRM
Revaluing a portfolio under severe but plausible scenarios, rather than under the distribution of ordinary days.
Style driftCFAFRM
A manager gradually moving away from the strategy the mandate describes.
SuitabilityCFA
The requirement to judge an investment against the client’s objectives, constraints and circumstances before recommending or making it.
Sunk costCFA
A cost already incurred and unrecoverable, and therefore irrelevant to any decision now.
Supervised learningCFA
Machine learning that fits a model to examples with known outcomes — labelled data — so it can predict the outcome for new cases.
Survivorship biasCFAFRM
Distortion caused by studying only the entities that lasted long enough to appear in the sample.
Sustainable growth rateCFA
The rate at which a firm can grow without changing its capital structure or issuing new equity.
SwapCFAFRM
An agreement to exchange one stream of cash flows for another over time.
Systematic riskCFAFRM
Risk affecting the whole market, which cannot be diversified away.
Systemic riskFRM
The risk that the failure of one institution or market propagates through the system as a whole.

T

Tactical asset allocationCFA
Short-term deliberate deviation from the strategic allocation to exploit a perceived opportunity.
Tax-loss harvestingCFA
Realising losses deliberately to offset gains elsewhere, while keeping the portfolio’s economic exposure broadly intact.
ThetaCFAFRM
The change in an option’s value from the passage of one day, all else equal.
Time value of moneyCFA
The principle that a sum available today is worth more than the same sum later, because today’s sum can be invested.
Time-weighted returnCFA
The compound growth of one unit of currency in a portfolio, with the effect of external cash flows removed by chain-linking sub-period…
Tracking errorCFA
The standard deviation of the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns.
TrancheCFAFRM
One slice of a securitisation with a defined priority of payment, so that losses hit the junior slices before the senior ones.
Treynor ratioCFA
Excess return per unit of systematic risk, using beta in the denominator.
Two and twentyCFA
The classic alternative fee model: an annual management fee of around 2% of assets plus around 20% of profits.
Type I errorCFAFRM
Rejecting a true null hypothesis.
Type II errorCFAFRM
Failing to reject a false null hypothesis.

U

Unexpected lossFRM
The volatility of credit losses around the expected level, which economic capital is held to absorb.
Unsupervised learningCFA
Machine learning that finds structure in data without a target variable, for example by grouping similar observations into clusters.
Unsystematic riskCFAFRM
Risk specific to one company or sector, which diversification can remove.

V

Value at RiskCFAFRM
The loss that will not be exceeded over a stated horizon with a stated confidence — for example, a 1-day 99% VaR of £1m means losses…
VarianceCFAFRM
The average squared deviation from the mean — the standard measure of dispersion.
VegaCFAFRM
The change in an option’s value for a one point change in implied volatility.
Venture capitalCFA
Equity investment in early-stage companies with high failure rates and highly skewed outcomes.
Vintage yearCFA
The year a private fund makes its first investment, and the basis on which its performance is compared with peers.

W

Wealth transferCFA
Moving assets to the next generation or to charity, structured for tax efficiency and for the family’s intentions.
Weighted average cost of capitalCFA
The blended required return on a company’s capital, weighting each source by its share of the total at market value.
Working capitalCFA
Current assets less current liabilities — the short-term capital tied up in operations.
Working capital managementCFA
Managing receivables, payables, inventory and cash so the business has liquidity without tying up more capital than it needs.
Wrong-way riskFRM
Exposure to a counterparty that rises precisely as that counterparty’s creditworthiness falls.

Y

Yield curveCFAFRM
The relationship between yield and maturity for bonds of the same credit quality.
Yield curve strategyCFA
Positioning a bond portfolio for an expected change in the shape of the curve — bullet, barbell or ladder structures expressing different…
Yield to maturityCFAFRM
The single discount rate that equates a bond’s price to the present value of its cash flows.
