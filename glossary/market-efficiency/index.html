Market efficiency (EMH)

Also called efficient market hypothesis, EMH. A CFA term, defined in plain English with the distinction examiners test.

What it means

The proposition that prices already reflect available information, so consistently beating the market on that information is not possible.

The exam angle

Three forms: weak (past prices), semi-strong (all public information), strong (all information, public and private). Technical analysis fails under weak-form; fundamental analysis fails under semi-strong.

Where it appears in the syllabus

  • EquitiesCFA Level I · exam weight 11–14% · Market organisation, indexes, industry analysis, dividend models.
  • Portfolio ConstructionCFA Level I · exam weight 8–12% · Risk and return, the CAPM, IPS construction, behavioural biases.

Related terms

  • BetaThe sensitivity of an asset’s return to the market’s return — its share of systematic risk.
  • Capital asset pricing modelA model stating that an asset’s expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium.
  • Market-capitalisation-weighted indexAn index in which weights are proportional to constituents’ market values.
  • Required rate of returnThe minimum return an investor demands for holding an asset, given its risk.
  • Book valueThe accounting value of equity: assets less liabilities as reported.
